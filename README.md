# OCTC KES Faculty Guide

The KCTCS Essential Standards (KES) describe 21 minimum expectations for online courses. They cover course design, interaction and collaboration, assessment, and learner support. Use this guide to prepare for an OCTC course review, work through feedback, or check a course after you update it.

[Open the KCTCS Essential Standards Guide in SharePoint](https://kctcs.sharepoint.com/sites/KCTCSOnline/Shared%20Documents/Forms/AllItems.aspx?id=%2Fsites%2FKCTCSOnline%2FShared%20Documents%2FDownload%20Copy_KCTCS%20Essential%20Standards%20Guide.pdf) using your KCTCS account.

## How course review works at OCTC

1. **Review your own course during initial KES training.** Use the standards to see what’s easy for students to find and use. Revisit the standards as you make changes, such as adding a module or revising a major assignment.
2. **A volunteer peer reviewer looks at a sample of your course.** The OCTC KOLA administrator verifies the findings and prepares the report.
3. **Use your report to plan updates.** Start with the corrections section. Each correction points to a location, explains what the reviewer verified, and gives you a way to check your update. The full checklist shows the result for each standard.
4. **Request re-review as soon as your course is ready.** If the fixes are quick, make them right away and reply to your report email to request re-review. If the updates need more time, plan for re-review the following semester, or the next semester you offer the course online. You can also reply to ask for instructional design help.

## Four common course updates

### Give each module a useful overview

Whether you call it a module, week, section, or unit, give students an overview with three things.

- **Learning outcomes.** What should students be able to do by the end of this part of the course?
- **Instructional guidance.** What should students read, watch, or do, and in what order?
- **Why It Matters.** How does this work connect to a course goal, a later assignment, or something students will do outside class?

### Improve course accessibility

For OCTC review, the minimum is an overall accessibility score of **85% with zero red indicators**. We encourage **90% or higher**. Fix any red indicators and check the score again after you replace or update files.

Start with these common fixes.

- **Scanned PDFs.** Try selecting or searching for a word in the body text. If the pages are images, replace the scan with an accessible source file or run optical character recognition (OCR). Check that the recognized text is accurate, then check the document structure and reading order.
- **Word documents.** Use Word’s built-in Title and Heading styles so students can navigate by section. Use heading levels in order. In tables, designate the first row as a header row so students can tell what each column means.
- **Images in Word, PowerPoint, and course pages.** Add alt text that explains what an informative image contributes in that context. Mark purely decorative images as decorative where the tool supports it. For a diagram that shows a process or route, put the full explanation in nearby text.
- **PowerPoint slides.** Use built-in layouts and their title and content placeholders. Give each slide a descriptive title and use section-header layouts where they help students follow the presentation. Check reading order and alt text, especially if you’ve added images or text boxes.

### Give students a way to share feedback

Give students a way to tell you what helps them learn and where they get stuck, both during the course and after it ends. An open feedback discussion or a short built-in survey can work during the course. Point students to an end-of-course survey or another way to share feedback after they finish. Ask about something you can act on, such as “What was hard to find or understand this week?”

### Explain what earns full points on graded work (KES 3.1)

When an assignment calls for your judgment, tell students what a full-credit response or submission looks like before they begin. Match the amount of detail to the task.

- **Short discussion post.** A sentence may be enough. “A full-points post answers the prompt, uses one example from the reading, and explains how the example supports your point.”
- **ENG 102 research paper.** A detailed rubric can explain how you’ll grade the argument, use of sources, organization, and citations.

Share the grading information where students can find it and tell which assignments it applies to. That can be in the directions, the syllabus, an accessible attachment, or a link students can open. The Blackboard rubric tool is optional.

## Examples of course updates

### 1.1 Module student learning outcomes

At the start of a module, tell students what they should be able to do by the end of it. Connect that skill to a broader Course Learning Outcome. For example, “By the end of this module, you’ll be able to compare two sources and explain which one better supports your claim.”

### 1.8 Why It Matters

Give students a short explanation of how the material fits into the bigger picture. For example, “You’ll practice comparing sources now so you can choose stronger evidence for your research paper.”

### 1.16 Accessible pages and media

If a diagram shows steps or a sequence students need to follow, give them those details in text. For example, add a numbered list below a route diagram and use concise alt text to identify the diagram and point readers to the directions. Check that you can follow the route from the written directions alone.
